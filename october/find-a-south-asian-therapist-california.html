The short answer

To find a South Asian therapist in California, search directories that let you filter by culture and language, such as South Asian Therapists and Psychology Today, look at group practices that specialize in South Asian care, and consider telehealth, which lets you work with any California-licensed therapist while you are in the state. Check the clinician's licence, ask about their experience with South Asian families, and use a free consultation to feel the fit.

For many South Asian clients, the hardest part of therapy is not the first session. It is the months of hesitation before it, wondering whether a therapist will understand why you cannot just move out, why your parents' opinion of your partner matters so much, or why a promotion can feel like both pride and pressure. Having to explain your whole world before you can talk about what hurts is exhausting.

That is why so many people search for a South Asian therapist near me. This guide is a practical, California-wide walkthrough: where to look, how to find a Hindi or Punjabi speaking therapist, how to check a licence, what to ask, and what to do if there is no one nearby.

Why cultural fit matters

You do not need a therapist from your exact background, but many people find it easier with someone who shares or deeply understands it. A culturally informed therapist is likely to recognize, without a long explanation:

  • The weight of family sacrifice and the pressure to make it worthwhile
  • Log kya kahenge, and how community opinion shapes decisions
  • Marriage expectations, family involvement in dating and mixed-culture relationships
  • Living between two cultures and never feeling fully at home in either
  • Intergenerational patterns, and love expressed through worry or control

Most importantly, a good fit will not treat your culture as the problem. The goal is room for both truths: you can respect where you come from and still question what you inherited. Our South Asian therapy page explains our approach.

Where to look for South Asian therapists in California

Culturally specific directories

The South Asian Therapists directory lists clinicians who identify as South Asian or specialize in South Asian mental health. General directories such as Psychology Today also let you filter by language and by the communities a therapist serves. When you search for desi therapists or a BIPOC therapist, read profiles closely: listing a language is not the same as experience with the family dynamics you are bringing.

Group practices with a South Asian focus

Group practices can match you with a clinician rather than leaving you to guess from a profile. Word of mouth, carefully

Friends, cousins or community groups can be good sources of names. If privacy is a concern, you do not have to tell anyone you are looking. Many people search quietly and book online.

Finding a Hindi or Punjabi speaking therapist

For some clients, especially those who grew up speaking another language at home, therapy in that language reaches feelings that English does not. Some things simply sound different in the language your family used. Language-matched therapy can also help if you want to bring a parent into a session.

Salonia Singh works in English, Punjabi and Hindi, so if you are looking for a Hindi speaking therapist or a Punjabi speaking therapist in California, she may be a good fit. For other South Asian languages, such as Tamil, Telugu, Gujarati, Bengali or Urdu, use the language filters in directories, and ask during a consultation how comfortable the clinician is working in that language.

Checking licences and credentials

In California, therapists are licensed by state boards. LMFTs, LCSWs and LPCCs are licensed by the Board of Behavioral Sciences, which offers a public licence lookup, and psychologists are licensed by the Board of Psychology. You may also see associates, such as an AMFT, who are registered, pre-licensed clinicians working under supervision. Associates often offer lower rates and more availability. Our associates work under Salonia's clinical supervision.

For a full breakdown of licence types, see our guide to therapist vs psychologist.

Questions to ask in a consultation

  • Have you worked with South Asian or first-generation clients before? What did that work look like?
  • How do you approach family expectations when they conflict with what I want?
  • Can we work in Hindi, Punjabi or another language if I want to?
  • What approaches do you use, and why might they fit my goals?
  • What are your fees, and do you provide superbills?

Notice how you feel afterwards. Did you have to explain the basics, or did they get it quickly? That feeling is often the best guide. Our guide on how to choose a therapist has more on this.

If there is no one near you: telehealth

If you live somewhere without a South Asian therapist nearby, telehealth widens the map. In California, you can work with any therapist licensed in the state, as long as you are physically located in California during sessions. Many clients in smaller cities, or who prefer privacy from their local community, find this is the easiest route. Read more about online therapy in California.

Do I need a therapist who is South Asian?

Not necessarily. What matters most is a therapist who understands South Asian family and cultural dynamics and does not treat your culture as the problem. Many clients do find it easier with someone who shares their background.
