What Is Couples Therapy and How Does It Actually Work?

The short answer

Couples therapy is a structured form of therapy where both partners work with a trained clinician to understand the patterns that keep causing conflict or distance, and to build new ways of communicating, repairing and reconnecting. Common approaches include the Gottman Method and Emotionally Focused Therapy. It can help with recurring arguments, emotional distance, infidelity, premarital questions and major life changes, and it can be done in person or online.

Most couples do not come to therapy after one bad fight. They come after the same argument has played out dozens of times, or after something has broken trust in a way neither of them knows how to repair. Often one partner has been asking for help for a while, and the other has agreed, a little reluctantly, to give it a try.

People search for all kinds of things before they call, from "couple therapy cheating" to "is it too late for us". This guide explains what couples therapy actually is, what happens in sessions, how affair recovery works, and when premarital counseling or marriage therapy makes sense, with some attention to the family and cultural pressures many of our clients carry.

How couples therapy works

At our practice, couples therapy is designed for both partners to take part in the shared work. It usually unfolds in three broad steps:

  1. Assessment. A joint session to hear what brings you in, sometimes followed by a brief individual conversation with each partner, so the therapist understands both perspectives and history.
  2. Mapping the cycle. Together you identify the pattern that repeats, for example one partner pursues and the other withdraws, and the feelings underneath it.
  3. Changing the pattern. You practice new ways of talking about hard things, repairing after conflict, and turning toward each other's needs.

Sessions are 50 minutes. The therapist is not a referee or a judge. Their job is to help both of you feel safe enough to say what is really going on.

The Gottman Method and Emotionally Focused Therapy

Two of the most widely used approaches, and the ones our couples work draws on, complement each other well.

The Gottman Method

Based on decades of research by Drs. John and Julie Gottman, this approach gives couples practical tools to manage conflict and strengthen friendship. It names four communication patterns that tend to erode relationships: criticism, contempt, defensiveness and stonewalling. Couples learn gentler ways to raise issues, how to calm down when flooded, and how to make and accept repair attempts.

Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT)

EFT focuses on attachment: the deep need to feel that your partner is there for you. It helps couples see that the fight is often a protest against disconnection. As each partner learns to express the softer feelings underneath anger or withdrawal, the cycle loses its grip. Salonia Singh is trained in both the Gottman Method (Levels 1 and 2) and EFT.

Couple therapy after cheating: can trust be rebuilt?

Infidelity is one of the most painful reasons couples seek help, and one of the most common. There is no single path, but many couples do rebuild trust with support. Gottman's affair recovery work describes three broad phases that many therapists find helpful:

  • Atone. The partner who had the affair takes responsibility, answers questions honestly, and tolerates the hurt partner's pain without becoming defensive.
  • Attune. The couple explores what was happening in the relationship before the affair, without shifting blame, and learns to meet each other's needs.
  • Attach. The couple rebuilds closeness and intimacy, often including physical intimacy, at a pace that feels safe.

Not every couple chooses to stay together, and that is not a failure of therapy. Sometimes the work helps partners decide, clearly and respectfully, whether to recommit or separate. Either way, having a place to make that decision with less chaos can matter a great deal.

A note on safety. Couples therapy is not recommended when one partner is being physically hurt, threatened or controlled. In that situation, individual support comes first. The National Domestic Violence Hotline is available at 1-800-799-7233.

Premarital counseling and marriage therapy

Premarital counseling is not only for couples with problems. It is a chance to talk through the things that are hardest to raise in the excitement of an engagement: money, careers, children, religion, household roles, conflict styles and, very often, family. For South Asian and mixed-culture couples, it can include questions like how involved parents will be, whether you will live near or with family, how festivals and traditions will be shared, and what happens when one family's expectations clash with the other's.

Marriage therapy, for couples already married, covers similar ground with the added weight of shared history. Whether you have been together three years or thirty, a marriage therapist can help you understand how your relationship has changed and what each of you needs now. Our practice has particular experience with South Asian and mixed-culture relationships. Read more on our South Asian therapy page.

Relationship anxiety and when individual work helps

Sometimes the difficulty lives mostly in one partner, for example relationship anxiety that shows up as constant doubt, reassurance-seeking or fear of abandonment. Individual therapy can be a helpful companion to couples work, or a starting point on its own. Our guide on individual therapy vs couples therapy explains how to choose.

Frequently asked questions

Can couples therapy help after cheating?

Many couples rebuild trust after infidelity with support. Therapy often moves through taking responsibility, understanding what was happening in the relationship, and slowly rebuilding closeness. Some couples decide to separate, and therapy can help make that decision more clearly and respectfully.

Do both partners have to attend?

Couples therapy is designed for both partners to take part. If one partner is not ready, individual therapy can still help you understand your part of the pattern and decide what you want.

What is the difference between premarital counseling and marriage therapy?

Premarital counseling helps engaged or committed couples talk through expectations, such as money, children, family roles and conflict, before marriage. Marriage therapy helps married couples work through current difficulties and patterns that have built up over time.
