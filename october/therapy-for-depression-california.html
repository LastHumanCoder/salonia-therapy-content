The short answer

Depression is more than a bad week. Clinicians usually consider it when low mood, loss of interest, exhaustion or hopelessness last most of the day, nearly every day, for two weeks or more. It is treatable. Talk therapy, sometimes combined with medication prescribed by a doctor, helps many people feel like themselves again. In California you can start with a licensed therapist in person or online, and a free consultation is an easy first step.

Depression can be quiet. It does not always look like crying or staying in bed. For many high-functioning adults it looks like going to work, answering messages and showing up for family, while feeling flat, far away and tired in a way sleep does not fix. Things that used to bring joy feel like chores, and it is easy to tell yourself you are just being lazy or ungrateful.

You are not. If you have been searching for a therapist near me for depression, that search is a sign that part of you is ready for things to feel different. This guide covers the common signs, how talk therapy helps, how long depression tends to last, and how to support someone you love who is struggling.

Common signs of depression

Only a qualified professional can assess what you are going through, but these are experiences people often describe:

  • Persistent sadness, emptiness or numbness
  • Losing interest in things you used to enjoy
  • Changes in sleep or appetite, in either direction
  • Fatigue, slowed thinking or trouble concentrating
  • Feelings of worthlessness, guilt or being a burden
  • Irritability, especially with the people closest to you
  • Thoughts of death or of not wanting to be here

In some families and cultures, including many South Asian households, depression is more often described through the body: headaches, stomach trouble, constant tiredness. It may be called "stress" or "tension" rather than depression. Physical symptoms deserve a check with your doctor, and if emotional heaviness comes with them, therapy is worth exploring too.

How long does depression last?

There is no single answer to how long does depression last, and anyone who gives you a fixed number is guessing. It depends on the person, what is contributing to it, and whether they get support. Some episodes lift within a few months; others last longer, especially without treatment. Some people experience depression once, while for others it returns during stressful periods.

What is more useful to know is that depression is highly treatable, and many people start to notice changes within weeks to a few months of consistent care. Therapy also helps you recognize early warning signs, so if low periods return, you can respond sooner.

How talk therapy for depression helps

Talk therapy for depression is not only about venting, although being truly heard matters. It is structured, collaborative work toward specific changes. Approaches our clinicians commonly draw on include:

  • Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT). Noticing harsh or hopeless thought patterns and testing them against the evidence.
  • Behavioral activation. Gently rebuilding activities that bring energy and meaning, starting very small.
  • Psychodynamic therapy. Exploring how earlier experiences and relationships shape how you feel now.
  • Internal Family Systems (IFS). Working with the inner critic and the parts of you that have been carrying the most.
  • EMDR. Useful when depression is linked to trauma or painful memories.

Individual therapy for depression gives you a private space to work at your own pace, with goals you set together. Learn more about individual therapy at our practice.

Therapy and medication

For some people, the most helpful depression treatment combines therapy with medication. Our clinicians do not prescribe, so if you would like to explore medication, a psychiatrist or your primary care doctor is the right person to speak with. With your permission, your therapist can coordinate with them.

How to help someone who is depressed

If someone you love is struggling, it is natural to want to fix it. What tends to help more is steady presence. A few ideas for how to help someone who is depressed:

  • Listen more than you advise. "That sounds really heavy" can land better than a list of solutions.
  • Offer practical help. A meal, a ride or help with an overdue task can matter a great deal.
  • Encourage professional support. Offer to help them look for a therapist or sit with them while they book.
  • Stay in touch. Depression tells people they are a burden. Regular, low-pressure contact quietly says otherwise.
  • Take talk of suicide seriously. If they mention wanting to die, ask directly and connect them with 988 or emergency services.

Supporting someone else is tiring. Your own therapy can help you stay steady without burning out.

Frequently asked questions

How do I find a therapist near me for depression?

Look for a California-licensed clinician who lists depression as a specialty, check their approach and fees, and book a consultation to see whether you feel comfortable. Telehealth lets you choose from therapists anywhere in the state. We offer sessions in West Los Angeles, Pasadena and online.

How long does depression last?

It varies widely. Some episodes lift within a few months, while others last longer, especially without support. Depression is treatable, and many people begin to notice changes within weeks to a few months of consistent care.

Is talk therapy enough for depression?

For many people, talk therapy on its own is very helpful. Others benefit from combining therapy with medication prescribed by a doctor. Your therapist can help you think through what might fit, and coordinate with a prescriber if you choose.

What if I do not feel sad, just numb or exhausted?

That is very common. Depression often shows up as numbness, low energy, irritability or losing interest rather than sadness. If that sounds familiar and it has lasted a while, it is worth talking with a professional.

How can I help a family member who is depressed but will not get help?

Stay connected, listen without pushing, and offer practical help. Gently share that support exists and offer to help them find it. If they talk about wanting to die, take it seriously and contact 988 or emergency services. Your own therapy can also help you cope.
